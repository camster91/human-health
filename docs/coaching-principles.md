# Coaching principles

1. Safety and personal rules override progression and model output.
2. The Guide gives one coherent recommendation, not competing specialist opinions.
3. Recovery context can reduce, replace or defer training stress.
4. Preserve the purpose of a session, not rigid exercise names.
5. Prefer minimum useful work over all-or-nothing failure.
6. Progress conservatively from repeated measured evidence.
7. Explain important changes in plain language.
8. Keep user override available.
9. Observed facts, user reports, preferences, inferences and external evidence are distinct.
10. Never silently treat unlike exercises/equipment as equivalent.
11. Do not diagnose, prescribe medication/insulin, provide emergency monitoring, or clear injuries.
12. Professional escalation is a valid recommendation.
13. Learn from behaviour without hidden irreversible changes.
14. Maintenance is success when it matches the person's life and goals.
15. Do not make every available health signal visible; synthesize it into useful action.
16. Use current trusted evidence when needed and keep provenance.
17. No guilt, streak punishment or fake universal health score.
18. The correct interface may be a card, video, body map, chart, timer, map, voice cue or short explanation—not necessarily chat.
